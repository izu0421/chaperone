Noradrenaline is a small-molecule soluble ligand and GPR85 is an orphan 7TM GPCR, so PLA and co-IP don't apply (no protein partner to pull down). A functional readout in receptor-expressing cells directly tests whether noradrenaline activates GPR85, beyond what a docked pose from AF3 can show.
Target tissue(s)
brain, retina, skeletal muscle
Why these tissues (HPA)
HPA RNA shows GPR85 enriched in brain (19.1 nTPM), retina (11.2 nTPM) and skeletal muscle (29.7 nTPM). Brain and retina are also noradrenergic-innervated, so they are physiologically plausible. HPA has no data for noradrenaline, which is a metabolite, so the ligand side relies on known neurotransmitter biology. The assay itself is done in a heterologous cell line (HEK293), with native-tissue follow-up second.
Cell type(s)
Retinal amacrine cells, Retinal bipolar cells, Retinal horizontal cells
Protocol notes
1) Express human GPR85 (N-terminal signal sequence/HA or FLAG tag, confirm surface expression by flow cytometry or ELISA) in HEK293 cells. Use mock-transfected cells as the negative control. Because GPR85 is an orphan with unknown G-protein coupling, run several readouts in parallel: a Gs cAMP assay (GloSensor or HTRF), Gi (forskolin-suppressed cAMP), Gq (Ca2+ flux or IP1), and β-arrestin recruitment (PRESTO-Tango or BRET). Alternatively, use a promiscuous Gα16 or chimeric Gα (Gqi5, Gqs5) system. 2) Run a noradrenaline dose-response from 1 nM to 100 µM, with ascorbate/antioxidant in the buffer to prevent catecholamine oxidation. Test structurally related catecholamines (dopamine, adrenaline, L-DOPA), a non-catechol amine (tyramine) and a non-related amine as specificity controls. 3) Test a pocket-mutant arm: alanine mutants of the AF3-predicted contact residues. Loss of response would support the predicted pose. Optionally add direct binding by radioligand ([3H]-noradrenaline) or label-free methods (SPR/DSF on purified receptor, or a cellular thermal shift assay). 4) Follow-up in native tissue: use GPR85-expressing retinal or brain cultures, or iPSC neurons, with GPR85 knockdown/knockout to check response dependence.
Controls
Positive control: β2-adrenergic receptor (cAMP) or α1A (Ca2+) stimulated with noradrenaline, which validates the assay and the ligand batch. Negative controls: mock/parental HEK293 cells, an irrelevant orphan GPCR expressed at a similar level, vehicle with ascorbate, and adrenergic antagonists (propranolol, prazosin, yohimbine) to rule out endogenous HEK adrenergic receptors (HEK293 expresses endogenous β2). Include a GPR85 pocket-mutant and a non-catechol ligand as specificity controls.
Caveats
The verdict is LIKELY_PROMISCUOUS_POCKET. A high ipTM (0.93) is expected for a small catecholamine placed in any GPCR bundle and is not evidence of specific binding. The ChEMBL lookups failed, so prior activity at GPR85 is unknown rather than absent. The screen is unstable: only 2 of 11 receptors were confident, and the 0.93/0.92 vs 0.90/0.92 folds straddle the 0.91 gate. GPR85 is a true orphan with unknown G-protein coupling, so a negative result in one pathway doesn't rule out signalling in another. Endogenous adrenergic receptors in host cells can cause false positives. Catecholamine oxidation can produce artefacts. HPA gives no protein-level data or subcellular location for GPR85 (RNA only), and none for noradrenaline. Non-neuronal expression (spermatids, Kupffer cells, Hofbauer cells) suggests a more complex biology.
