Rationale
The contradiction is valid: CD69's UniProt FUNCTION annotation describes carbohydrate/sialic-acid-dependent binding as a core physiological mechanism, not a peripheral PTM elsewhere in the protein — this is analogous to the galectin caveat, meaning CD69's native recognition mode may be fundamentally glycan-mediated rather than a bare CTLD-CTLD protein contact. Since fold_evidence_status is not_run, there is no actual AF3 structure here to argue "the interface residues are PTM-free" from in the first place, so the original argument that a protein-mediated contact remains plausible because no glycosylation site landed on the interface residues is unsupported — no interface was even modeled to check. Given CD69 has a documented, explicit carbohydrate-dependent binding mechanism (as with galectin-1), and CLEC2B is itself a heavily N-glycosylated CTLD (3 sites) that could plausibly serve as the ligand for that lectin-like recognition rather than a direct protein-protein CTLD interface, this pair should be flagged for the glycan-mediated artifact concern rather than confirmed as a novel bare-protein interaction. known_interaction_strength=none and hpa_plausibility=plausible are unchanged from before — co-expression in NK/myeloid cells still supports these proteins could meet, but the mechanism of interaction, if real, plausibly requires glycans that AF3 cannot represent.
PTM / glycosylation

CLEC2B carries 3 extracellular N-glycosylation sites on its ectodomain as well, reinforcing that a glycan-dependent mechanism is plausible for this pair rather than a pure protein-protein CTLD contact.
